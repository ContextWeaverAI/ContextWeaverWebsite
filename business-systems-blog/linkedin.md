A modern car has more than 50 computers inside it. The engine controller, the ABS, the transmission, the airbags. Not one of them knows it is in a car.

That is also how your factory runs the business around the line.

Part 5 of Deconstructing the Smart Factory: the systems everyone actually logs into. ERP for the money and materials. CRM for the customer. CMMS for the machines. PLM for the product design. QMS for the quality record. Each is decades of hardening, and each is genuinely excellent at its own job. You cannot run a manufacturing business without them.

Here is the catch. They sit side by side, at the same level, and not one of them can see the other four.

So when something goes wrong on the line, the answer exists. It is just cut into five pieces. The machine that drifted is in the CMMS. The recipe that changed is in PLM. The quality hold is in QMS. The order and the cost are in ERP. The customer now waiting is in CRM. Every piece is true, and no system holds the piece next to it.

Five perfect records. Five different names for the same steel. No shared identity between them.

A car full of computers that cannot talk to each other is not a car, it is a parking lot. The fix in the car is a bus that reads across all of them. A plant needs the same thing.

Name it. Remember it. React to it. Record the work. Run the business around it. Five systems, then six, then more, and still nothing that reads across them all.

What does? That is the last part.

Deconstructing the Smart Factory, Part 5.
